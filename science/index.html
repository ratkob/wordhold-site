The science

Wordhold is built on a few of the best-studied ideas in memory research. This page names the studies.

An honest note first: these studies tested the methods, not this app. Nobody has measured how many words Wordhold readers remember, so we don't make claims like "remember 90% of your words". What we can say is which methods the app uses and where the evidence for them comes from.

Spaced repetition: each word comes back just before you'd forget it

Reviewing something spread out over time beats cramming it into one sitting. In a study of more than 1,350 people learning facts, Cepeda and colleagues found that the best gap between study sessions grows with how long you want to remember: the further away the test, the longer the ideal gap. Wordhold spaces every word's reviews further apart each time you remember it.

Active recall: test yourself instead of re-reading

Trying to remember something strengthens memory more than reading it again. Rowland's review of 61 studies found a clear benefit of testing over restudying (an average effect size of about g = 0.50). That's why each card shows the word and its sentence first and asks you to recall the meaning before you reveal it.

Scheduling by FSRS

FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) is the open-source algorithm used by modern Anki. It models how quickly each memory fades and picks the day each word should come back. It grew out of the research of Ye, Su and Cao, who trained a memory model on millions of real review logs and used it to find review schedules that cost the least effort. Wordhold schedules reviews to aim for about 90% recall.

Words in the sentence where you met them

Reading a word in context and studying its definition work better together than either does alone. Bolger, Balass, Landen and Perfetti found that definitions convey meaning better than context alone, and that meeting a word in several different sentences builds a more general sense of it than one repeated sentence. Stahl and Fairbanks' earlier review of vocabulary teaching studies also found that methods combining definitions and context did best. So each card shows your book's sentence alongside the definition, and a second example sentence for variety.

More encounters help

Across studies of learning words from reading, Uchihara, Webb and Yanagisawa found that the more often learners met a word, the more likely they were to learn it (a correlation of about r = .34), though the effect varied a lot between studies. Wordhold gives each word repeated, scheduled encounters instead of leaving it to chance.

Recalling with the right cue

Memories come back most easily when the cue you have at recall matches what was there when you learned (Tulving and Thomson's encoding specificity principle). Showing the original sentence from your book on the card is a cue that matches the moment you first met the word.

What we don't claim

We don't say the app is "scientifically proven", promise a number of words in a number of days, or tailor anything to "learning styles". A major review by Pashler and colleagues found no good evidence that teaching to a person's preferred learning style improves learning.

Privacy policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

The short version: Wordhold has no accounts and shows no ads, and we never sell your data. Your words live on your iPhone and in your own iCloud, where we can't read them; the one exception is the optional first-day screen recording described below, which can show your word lists. Photos are read on your phone and never uploaded. Unless you turn it off (and in the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland, only if you agree first), counts of how the app is used, crash reports and which ad brought you here go to a few services (PostHog, Google Firebase and Singular), tied to a random ID for your phone, not your name. You can stop that at any time in Settings › Share usage data. Once Wordhold sells subscriptions, RevenueCat records your subscription, whatever you choose about usage data.

Who we are

Wordhold is made by Ratko Bozovic, an independent developer ("we", "us"). We are responsible for the personal data described here (the "controller"). Contact: wordhold@ratko.biz

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your words, books, sentences and practice history. Stored in the app on your phone. We don't receive them, except that the optional first-day screen recording can show your word lists (see below).
  • A spare copy of your words. Wordhold keeps its own copy of your words, with their books and practice history, in a file on your phone, so it can offer them back if iCloud removes them (for example when you sign out of iCloud or switch it off for Wordhold). The copy stays on your phone and is deleted with the app.
  • Photos of word lists and book pages. The text is read on your phone by Apple's text recognition. The photo is then thrown away; only the words you choose, and the sentence they came from, are kept. Photos are never uploaded to us or anyone else. If you pick a photo from your library instead of taking one, Wordhold sees only that photo, not the rest of your library.
  • Picking the right meaning of a word. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence (iOS 26 or later), Apple's language model, running on your phone, reads the sentence a word was saved with and chooses which of the dictionary's meanings it uses. This runs entirely on your phone: the sentence and the word are never sent anywhere, and the app keeps only which meaning was chosen. You can always pick another meaning yourself.
  • Whether you are 18 or over. Two topics, curse words and sexual vocabulary, are shown only to adults. If you ask to see them (on iOS 26 or later), your iPhone asks whether to share your age range with Wordhold. Wordhold learns only whether you are 18 or over, never your age or date of birth, keeps that one answer on your phone, and never sends it to us or anyone else.
  • The dictionary. Built into the app, so looking up a word sends nothing anywhere.
  • Reminders, widgets and Spotlight search. Reminders are scheduled on your phone; the widget and Spotlight read from the app's storage on your phone.

iCloud sync (Apple)

If iCloud is on for Wordhold, your words, books and practice history are synced through Apple's CloudKit, in the private database of your own iCloud account. Only you can read it; we, as the developer, cannot. This data is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy and counts toward your iCloud storage. You can turn sync off in your iPhone's iCloud settings.

Wordhold also keeps a small note of the day you first opened it during the free launch, in your iCloud and in your iPhone's Keychain (which keeps it even if you delete the app), so you keep everything free on a new iPhone or after reinstalling (see Support). This note is a date and nothing else, and we can't see it.

Siri and Shortcuts

When you add a word with Siri, Apple processes your voice request and hands the app the recognised text. Apple's handling of Siri requests is covered by Apple's Siri privacy notice.

Book covers (Open Library)

To find a book's cover and author, the app searches Open Library (run by the Internet Archive): while you type a book's title (from the third letter, once you pause), and later in the background for books that still have no cover, including books added by a scan or from another device. Each search sends only the book's title, the app's version and, like any web request, your IP address. Covers are then downloaded from covers.openlibrary.org, which sees only the cover's number and, like any web request, your IP address, and stored on your phone.

Usage data, crash reports and ad measurement (you can turn this off)

To learn what helps, fix crashes and see which of our ads work, Wordhold can send the following. None of it includes your words, sentences, book titles, notes, anything you type, or your photos, with one exception: the first-day screen recording described below.

Your choice

If your iPhone's region is set to a country in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland (or to no region), the app asks you first, and nothing below is sent until you tap Share usage data. Everywhere else it is on from the start. Wherever you are, you can turn it off at any time in Settings › Share usage data. Turning it off stops PostHog, Firebase and Singular, deletes crash reports still waiting on your phone, and stops RevenueCat passing your purchases to Singular. It does not undo what was already sent; to have that deleted, see Your rights.

A random install ID

Wordhold makes a random ID on your phone the first time it runs. It isn't made from anything about you, but it is an identifier, so we treat it as personal data. PostHog, Firebase and Singular receive it, and RevenueCat stores it with your subscription while usage data is shared, so the same ID links what each service has. You can see it in Settings › About › Install ID; quote it if you ask us about your data. The services also add their own IDs (listed below).

PostHog: how the app is used

PostHog receives a short list of named events, such as a practice session finished, a list scanned or a step of the welcome pages left. Each event carries only counts, choices and yes or no, never a word, sentence or book title. PostHog adds the app version, iOS version, iPhone model, its own device ID, your IP address, and a rough location (country or city) that it works out from your IP address. Our PostHog project is on PostHog's servers in the United States. See PostHog's privacy policy.

Screen recordings on the first day. On about 3 in 10 new installs, PostHog also records the screen during the first day, as a series of screenshots, so we can see where people get stuck. Photos, the camera, what you type, practice cards and word pages are hidden. Lists of your words and books, with their sentences, can be visible (for example the Words list, Missed this week, and the list you check after a scan). Recordings go only to PostHog, with the same consent as the events.

Google Firebase Analytics: how the app is used, and our Google Ads

Firebase Analytics (Google) receives the same events as PostHog, and whether onboarding was finished or a free week or subscription started. Firebase adds its own automatic events (first open, sessions, app updates), the device model, iOS and app version, its own app instance and installation IDs, and a rough location that Google works out from your IP address. We use it to improve the app and to measure which of Wordhold's own Google Ads bring readers. It is never used to show you ads: ads personalisation, ad storage and ad user data are always turned off in Google's consent settings, and Wordhold gives Google no advertising ID (IDFA) and no identifier for vendor (IDFV). No screen recordings go to Google.

Google Firebase Crashlytics: crash reports

When Wordhold crashes, Firebase Crashlytics receives a report of where in the code it happened, the device model, iOS version, free memory and storage at the time, and the last few Firebase events, tied to the install ID. We use it only to fix crashes.

Google processes Firebase data on its servers, which may be outside the EU. See Google's privacy policy and Firebase's privacy and security page.

Singular: which ad brought you here

Singular measures Wordhold's ads. It receives the same events as PostHog (no recordings), each install and app open, the install ID, its own device ID, your iPhone's ID for Wordhold's maker (IDFV), your IP address, device model and iOS version, and Apple's ad attribution token. Once Wordhold sells subscriptions, RevenueCat also passes Singular your trial starts, purchases, renewals and refunds. Singular matches all this to ad clicks and tells the ad networks which installs their ads brought. The ad networks we may advertise with are Apple Ads, Google Ads, Meta and TikTok; they receive this under their own privacy policies, as separate controllers, not as our processors.

Apple's tracking question. While usage data is shared, your iPhone asks once whether Wordhold may track your activity (App Tracking Transparency). Only if you tap Allow does Singular read your phone's advertising ID (IDFA) and tell ad networks that the install and purchases were yours. Until you answer, and if you tap Ask App Not to Track, Wordhold tells Singular (and RevenueCat's reports to it) to limit data sharing: Singular still receives the data above, but doesn't pass your installs and purchases on to ad networks as yours, and measures ads with Apple's anonymous install reports (SKAdNetwork and AdAttributionKit) and its own matching. You can change the answer in your iPhone's Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking. If you said no to usage data, you are never asked. See Singular's privacy policy.

Subscriptions (RevenueCat)

While Wordhold is free (the launch period), nothing is sold and RevenueCat isn't used.

Once Wordhold sells subscriptions, purchases are made through Apple, and we never see your payment details. We use RevenueCat to check whether you have an active subscription or free week and to count subscriptions. RevenueCat runs for everyone, whatever you choose about usage data, because it records what you paid for. It receives a random ID it makes itself, your purchase history from Apple (product, price, dates, free week, renewals, cancellations, refunds), your App Store country and your IP address. While usage data is shared, it also stores the install ID and Singular's device ID, and passes your purchases on to Singular as described above. RevenueCat processes data in the United States. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

When you email us

If you write to wordhold@ratko.biz, we receive your email address, your name if your email shows it, and what you write. Settings › Send feedback also fills in the app version, your iPhone model and iOS version, which you can delete before sending. We use these only to answer you. Mail to this address is forwarded by Cloudflare to a Gmail inbox (Google). We keep emails for two years, or until you ask us to delete them.

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in. The app never asks for your name, email address or phone number.
  • No ads inside Wordhold.
  • We never sell or rent your data.
  • No precise location and no contacts. The camera is used only while you photograph a word list or page.

Why we may use this data (EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland)

  • Usage data, crash reports, screen recordings and ad measurement (PostHog, Firebase, Singular): your consent, asked before anything is sent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings › Share usage data.
  • Subscriptions (RevenueCat): to perform our contract with you, that is, to give you what you paid for, and to keep the records tax law requires.
  • Book covers (Open Library): our legitimate interest in showing your books with their covers. Only the title is sent, with the app's version.
  • Emails you send us: our legitimate interest in answering you.

PostHog, Google, Singular and RevenueCat process data for us as our processors, under data processing agreements that require them to protect it at least as well as this policy describes. Some of them process it in the United States. PostHog, Google and Singular are certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (and its UK and Swiss extensions), and their data processing terms also include the EU standard contractual clauses. RevenueCat's data processing terms include the EU standard contractual clauses, with the UK and Swiss addenda.

How long data is kept

Your words stay on your phone and in your iCloud until you delete them. Data held by the services is kept for:

  • PostHog: usage events for up to 1 year; screen recordings for 30 days.
  • Firebase Analytics: detailed data tied to the install ID for 2 months; after that only totals that don't identify you remain.
  • Firebase Crashlytics: crash reports for 90 days.
  • Singular: data tied to your phone for up to about 2 months, and up to 6 months for installs that came from a Google ad.
  • RevenueCat (once subscriptions are sold): while we use RevenueCat, and as long as tax and accounting rules require.
  • Support emails: two years, or until you ask us to delete them.

Your choices and rights

  • Stop sharing usage data: Settings › Share usage data in the app.
  • Stop ad tracking: your iPhone's Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.
  • Export your words at any time from the app's Settings, free.
  • Delete your words: deleting the app removes your words and the spare copy from your phone. Up to two small markers stay in your iPhone's Keychain, so a reinstall remembers them: the day you first opened Wordhold during the free launch, if you did, and whether you already used the free first import. They hold no words, and they are removed when you erase the iPhone. To delete the iCloud copy, open your iPhone's Settings, tap your name, then iCloud › Manage Account Storage (or Manage Storage), choose Wordhold and delete its data.
  • See or delete what the services hold: we don't know who you are, so we need an ID to find your records. Email wordhold@ratko.biz with the Install ID from Settings › About. A request covers what is linked to that ID in PostHog, Firebase, Singular and RevenueCat, purchases and free weeks included. Purchases made while usage data was off are kept by RevenueCat under its own ID, which the app doesn't show; for those, include the order ID from Apple's purchase receipt email.
  • If you are in the EU, EEA, UK or Switzerland you have the right to access, correct or delete your personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, to withdraw consent at any time, and to complain to a data protection authority: in Spain, where we are based, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, or the one where you live.
  • Wordhold makes no decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects.

Children

Wordhold is suitable for all ages but is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal data from them. The app collects no names or contact details from anyone.
